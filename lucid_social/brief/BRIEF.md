## Compliance guardrails (US dietary supplement marketing: FDA labeling rules + FTC substantiation + Meta policy)
- Structure/function wording only ("supports healthy testosterone levels*", "testosterone support*"). Any post or caption that
  makes one carries the asterisk and the FDA disclaimer (short form on the graphic, full text in the caption).
- No disease or drug claims: low T / hypogonadism, ED / sexual performance, fertility, TRT replacement, "cure", "treat",
  "prevent", "clinically proven" for the finished product, percentages or speed of results, before/after.
- Ingredient facts are fine when literal (names, amounts, standardisation, origin, traditional use as history). "Clinically
  studied" may be said of a branded ingredient only if the supplier's studies exist: mark it TBD-verify.
- Meta personal-attributes rule (matters if posts are boosted later): never assert or imply the viewer's age, health
  condition or body ("Struggling with low T?", "Men over 40, ..."). Speak about the product, the protocol, the standard.
- "For adult men only" where the product is shown as a recommendation.
- Avoid "boost", "skyrocket", "alpha", "manliness" bro-tone; the brand is calm, classical, credible, premium.
